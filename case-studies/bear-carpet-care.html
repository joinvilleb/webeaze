The challenge

Nearly every carpet cleaning website ends at the same wall: "call for a free estimate." The homeowner has no idea whether this is a $90 job or a $400 one, so they open three more tabs and call whoever answers first. For a family business that has been cleaning carpet in Harrisburg for over thirty years, being one of those three tabs is a bad place to be.

Bear Carpet Care already had the reputation. Dave and the team have Google reviews describing carpets with six dogs' worth of wear coming back looking brand new. The problem was never the work. It was that a stranger comparing cleaners at 9pm could not tell any of them apart.

What we did

We built the site around the one question everybody has and nobody answers. Prices go on the page, in public, with the room sizes they cover and a plain note about what gets quoted separately. Everything else on the site supports that decision.

Prices, published

Two rooms at $79.95, four at $149.95, upholstery by the piece, rugs at 15% off, with the square footage each covers.

What they actually clean

Carpet, upholstery and oriental rugs, each with its method, its starting price, and its own page.

Real Google reviews

Named, dated, five-star reviews pulled onto the page instead of hidden behind a link nobody clicks.

Tap to call, always

The phone number is a button in the hero, in the nav, and pinned on mobile. Booking is a phone call, so the site makes that the easy thing.

An FAQ that answers locals

Cost, service area, pet safety, and rug pick-up, written the way people actually type them into Google.

Thirty years, said plainly

Family-owned, locally operated, non-toxic and pet-safe products. The trust signals a national chain cannot claim.
