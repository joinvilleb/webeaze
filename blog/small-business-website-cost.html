How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost?

WebEaze Team June 16, 2026 7 min read
Quick Answer

Small business website costs range from $0 (DIY, your time only) to $15,000 or more (custom agency). Most small businesses do best with a managed monthly plan at $100 to $300 per month, which includes design, hosting, updates, and support with no large upfront cost.

If you have searched for website pricing for your small business, you have probably found everything from free to tens of thousands of dollars. Both are real, and both tell part of the story. This guide breaks down every option honestly so you can decide what is right for your business.

Option 1: DIY Website Builders

Platforms like Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy, and Weebly let you build your own website using drag-and-drop tools. On paper, this is the cheapest option.

What you pay: $16 to $49 per month on a paid plan, plus a domain name ($15 to $25 per year).

What you actually get: A template-based site that you design, build, write, photograph, troubleshoot, and update yourself, forever.

The hidden cost nobody talks about is your time. A business owner who spends 20 hours building their website is spending 20 hours not doing the work that actually earns them money. And when something breaks, looks wrong on mobile, or needs updating six months from now, that is on you to fix.

DIY builders also have significant SEO limitations. The templates are not optimized for local search, which means the site you spend hours building may never rank in Google for your target customers.

Best for: Businesses with essentially no budget and plenty of time to learn the tools.

Option 2: Hiring a Freelancer

A freelance web designer builds the site for you and hands it over when done.

What you pay: $500 to $5,000 upfront, depending on experience and complexity. Budget $1,500 to $3,000 for a professional result.

What you actually get: A website built to your specs, typically in 2 to 6 weeks. After that, you are mostly on your own.

The catch is what happens after launch. Most freelancers do not include ongoing support. When you need a page added, a price changed, or a new service listed, you hire them again at $50 to $150 per hour. Hosting, security updates, and backups are usually your responsibility to manage separately.

Quality also varies significantly. A $500 freelancer and a $5,000 freelancer produce very different results. Without seeing their portfolio and understanding what SEO they include, you are taking a chance.

Best for: Businesses that want a custom site, have someone in-house who can manage it afterward, and have the budget for the upfront cost.

Option 3: A Web Design Agency

Agencies offer strategy, design, development, and sometimes SEO, handled by a full team.

What you pay: $3,000 to $15,000 or more upfront, plus $100 to $500 per month for ongoing hosting and maintenance.

What you actually get: A polished, professionally built website with a structured process and clear deliverables.

The downside for most small businesses is the price point. A $10,000 website project is a significant investment, and many agencies also lock you into long-term contracts for ongoing support.

Best for: Established businesses with larger budgets, e-commerce stores, or organizations that need custom functionality.

Option 4: The Monthly Managed Plan

Instead of a large upfront cost, you pay a single monthly fee that covers everything: professional design, hosting, updates, security, and support.

What you pay: WebEaze plans start at $169 per month for the Essential plan and $249 per month for the Growth plan. No setup fee, no long-term contract required.

What you actually get: A professionally designed website built for your specific business, live in 5 to 14 business days. Unlimited content updates so your site stays accurate. Fast hosting with an SSL security certificate. A team you can reach whenever something needs to change.

This model works particularly well for small service businesses (contractors, salons, restaurants, medical offices, lawn care, cleaning services) that want a professional online presence without managing technology themselves.

Hidden Costs That Catch People Off Guard

No matter which option you choose, here are costs many business owners do not budget for:

  • Domain name: $15 to $25 per year. You need this regardless of who builds your site.
  • SSL certificate (HTTPS): Free with most modern hosts, but some charge up to $100 per year. Always confirm before signing up.
  • Website hosting: $10 to $50 per month if not included in your plan.
  • Content updates: With freelancers and most agencies, future changes cost $50 to $150 per hour.
  • Professional photos: A website looks dramatically better with real photos. Budget $300 to $800 for a basic shoot.
  • Local SEO: A website alone does not guarantee Google rankings. SEO services typically run $200 to $500 per month separately. With WebEaze Growth, this is included.

Which Option Is Right for Your Business?

If you are just starting out with almost no budget: A DIY builder gets you online for minimal cost, but plan to upgrade when the business picks up.

If you want a professional result without ongoing technical headaches: A managed monthly plan like WebEaze gives you professional quality, no upfront cost, and zero technology to manage yourself.

If you have complex needs like e-commerce, membership areas, or custom integrations: A dedicated agency with the right budget is likely the better fit.

For the vast majority of small service businesses, the managed monthly model delivers the best value. You get professional quality, no technical work on your end, and predictable monthly costs that make it easy to budget.
