Does My Small Business Need a Website?

WebEaze Team
April 28, 2026 6 min read
Quick Answer

Yes. 97% of consumers search for local businesses online before deciding where to spend their money. Without a website, you are invisible to most of your potential customers, no matter how good your product or service is.

If you have been putting off building a website for your small business, you are not alone. Many business owners assume that social media is enough, that word of mouth will carry them, or that a website is something to worry about later. But the data tells a different story.

These are not abstract statistics. Every time someone searches for a plumber, a salon, a landscaper, or a restaurant in your area, they are going online first. If your business does not have a website, you are not in that conversation.

Why Social Media Is Not a Substitute

This is the most common objection we hear: "I already have a Facebook page and an Instagram. Isn't that enough?"

It is not, and here is exactly why:

  • Facebook controls who sees your posts. Organic reach for business pages has fallen below 5% of your followers. You are publishing to an audience that mostly never sees it.
  • Social profiles do not rank in Google for service searches. When someone searches "roof repair near me" or "hair salon in Wilmington," Google shows websites, not Facebook pages.
  • You do not own a social media profile. If the platform changes its rules, reduces reach, or gets shut down, your entire online presence disappears overnight.
  • You cannot fully control the experience. Your brand, your story, your pricing, your booking flow, all of it is constrained by what the platform allows.

Social media is excellent for building awareness and staying connected with customers. But it works best when it drives people back to your website.

What a Website Actually Does for Your Business

A professional website is not just an online brochure. When it is built and optimized correctly, it works for you around the clock.

It shows up in Google when people search for what you offer

A website with proper local SEO can get your business into Google search results and the local map pack, which is the set of three business listings that appear at the top of local searches. Those three spots get the majority of clicks. Without a website, you cannot compete for them.

It establishes credibility in under three seconds

Studies consistently show that people form an opinion about your business within three seconds of landing on your website. A professional, well-designed site signals that you are established, trustworthy, and worth calling. A missing or outdated website signals the opposite.

It answers questions before they have to call

Most potential customers want to know your hours, your pricing range, what services you offer, and what your work looks like before they pick up the phone. A website answers all of those questions automatically, filtering out bad-fit customers and converting good-fit ones.

It captures leads while you sleep

A contact form, a booking button, or a free consultation link works 24 hours a day. You could wake up tomorrow to a new inquiry that came in at 11pm from someone who found you on Google.

What About Google Business Profile?

Google Business Profile (the free listing that shows up on Google Maps) is an essential tool for local businesses, and every business should have one. But it works best when paired with a website.

Your Google Business Profile tells people you exist. Your website tells them why they should choose you over everyone else. When someone finds you on the map and clicks "website," that is your opportunity to close them. Without a website, that opportunity vanishes.

Google also uses your website as a trust signal when deciding how to rank your business in local search. Businesses with a well-built, relevant website consistently outrank competitors who rely on a Google Business Profile alone.

Let's put this in concrete terms. If your type of business gets searched 500 times per month in your area, and you have no website so you capture none of that traffic, and just 5 of those searchers would have become customers at an average value of $200 each, that is $1,000 per month you are not capturing.
